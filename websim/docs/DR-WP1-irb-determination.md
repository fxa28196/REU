## 1. The determination

The faculty mentor determined that **no IRB review is required for this project.**

The reasoning, exactly as reported, is two grounds:

1. **The work does not involve human subjects.**
2. **The work is not yet a real-world application.**

The researcher relayed this on 2026-08-02, in these terms: *"prof said no irb, not real
peopple wort be real life application yet."*

That is the whole of what was determined and the whole of the reasoning available to this
record. **No legal or regulatory interpretation is added here.** This document does not
assess the determination against 45 CFR 46, does not characterise the project as "exempt"
versus "not human subjects research" (those are different findings under the regulations and
the report distinguishes neither), and does not restate the reasoning in stronger or more
technical language than the mentor's own.

## 3. The one condition, which comes from the mentor's own reasoning

The second ground — *not yet a real-world application* — is a statement about the project's
current use, and the word *yet* is the mentor's. Read plainly, that scopes the
determination:

> **This determination covers the project as it is used today: an academic simulation study,
> for research and for the REU deliverables. A genuine real-world deployment — a tool used by
> an agency, an outreach team, or anyone making actual decisions about actual people — is a
> materially different question, and the determination must be re-raised with the mentor
> before that happens.**

This is not an additional condition imposed by this record. It is what the reasoning already
says: a ground that rests on "not yet" stops supporting the conclusion when the "yet"
arrives. It is written down so that a future contributor who reaches for this document as
blanket cover finds the boundary in the same place the mentor put it.

**Trigger to re-raise:** any move from research use toward operational use. Deploying the
browser port as a public-facing decision aid, handing it to a service provider, or
presenting outputs as guidance for siting or dispatch would each cross that line. Publishing
a paper, a chapter, or a conference presentation about the simulation would not.
